# Editorial Lanes

## Worldview

The artist earns attention through a recurring way of seeing.

Strong sources:

- a belief the artist consistently lives;
- a contradiction they keep noticing;
- a social ritual or status game they can name precisely;
- a hard-won lesson from making music or living the work;
- a taste judgment with a real standard behind it;
- a specific moment that reveals a broader human truth;
- a useful connection between culture and the artist's emotional territory.

Weak sources:

- generic motivation;
- a random trending keyword;
- borrowed expertise;
- an opinion selected only because people may argue;
- advice the artist has not earned the right to give.

## Edge Test

Before softening a strong angle, ask:

1. What does this artist genuinely believe that some people will reject?
2. What standard, enemy, contradiction, or status game sits underneath it?
3. What would a generic artist be afraid to say here?
4. Is the thought documented in, or honestly inferred from, the artist's own truth?
5. Is it attacking an idea, behavior, or system rather than inventing claims about or targeting a vulnerable person?

Prefer the sharpest supportable phrasing. Do not confuse broad appeal with quality or disagreement with harm.

## Campaign-adjacent

Start from the song's emotional engine, not its title or marketing hook.

Map:

```text
song tension → artist belief → current human conversation → post worth reading alone
```

Example shape:

```text
A song about chasing unavailable love
→ artist repeatedly writes about confusing intensity with intimacy
→ current discussion about dating ambiguity
→ precise observation about the status reward hidden inside mixed signals
```

The post does not need to mention the song. Its job is to deepen the world the song belongs to.

## Direct release

This is allowed to sell, but it must remain specific and alive.

Strong direct release posts use one of:

- the sharpest lyric or emotional premise;
- a true making-of detail;
- a clear reason the song exists now;
- an approved clip, image, or single-art asset;
- a concrete invitation to listen, watch, save, or respond.

Do not pretend a direct release post is neutral commentary. Label it honestly.

## Angle Score

Score each proposed angle from 0-2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Artist fit | generic | plausible | unmistakably theirs |
| Truth | empty | familiar | precise and earned |
| Timeliness | stale/random | usable | genuinely relevant now |
| Distinctiveness | interchangeable | competent | memorable angle |
| Risk | unsupported/exploitative | repairable | clean and supportable |

Discard any angle with a 0 in Artist fit, Truth, or Risk. Prefer total scores of 8 or higher.

## Anti-Generic Rewrite

When a draft sounds generic, do not add louder adjectives. Find the missing observation.

Replace:

- abstraction with a moment;
- advice with what the artist noticed;
- a claim with the tension underneath it;
- a slogan with the cost of believing it;
- a release plug with a reason the song had to exist.

## Final Read-Aloud Test

Before adding a candidate to the slate:

1. Read it aloud in the artist's documented voice.
2. Remove any line that sounds like social media strategy talking.
3. Remove any question that exists only to fish for replies.
4. Verify factual wording against the source.
5. Confirm the post has one dominant thought.
